## 1. INSTAGRAM DM -- Cold Outreach (First Contact)

The goal is not to sell. The goal is to start a conversation. You are one coach reaching out to another. You built something. You want their honest take.

### Variation A -- Short (2-3 sentences)

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- been following your content for a bit, especially [SOMETHING SPECIFIC ABOUT THEM]. Quick question: how do you currently keep track of all your clients and programming? Curious because I built something for exactly that problem.

### Variation B -- Medium (4-5 sentences)

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- I came across your page through [SOMETHING SPECIFIC ABOUT THEM] and your approach to [THEIR STYLE/DISCIPLINE] really stood out to me. I'm a solo developer and I've been building a platform specifically for coaches like you -- the ones running everything themselves and juggling a dozen tools to do it. It handles clients, scheduling, payments, video libraries, programming -- all in one place. Not trying to pitch you, genuinely just looking for feedback from coaches who are actually in the trenches. Would you be open to a quick look?

### Variation C -- Detailed (full paragraph with context)

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- I've been following your work for a while now, and [SOMETHING SPECIFIC ABOUT THEM] honestly caught my attention because [REASON IT RESONATED]. I'm a solo founder building a platform called COACH-OS for independent [THEIR STYLE/DISCIPLINE] coaches. The whole idea came from watching coaches like you try to run a full business with a patchwork of spreadsheets, Google Drive, WhatsApp groups, and random apps that don't talk to each other. I built it so a solo coach can manage clients, track progress, schedule sessions, handle payments, store their video library, and actually see how their business is doing -- all from one place. I'm not a big company. It's just me. And I'm looking for a small group of founding coaches who want to shape what this becomes. No pressure at all -- but if you'd be open to a 15-minute call or even just a quick Loom walkthrough, I'd genuinely appreciate your perspective.

**Notes:**
- Never send the detailed version cold to someone who has never interacted with you. Start with A or B.
- Always personalize. Generic DMs get ignored or flagged.
- If they reply with anything positive, move to conversation -- do not immediately drop a link.

## 2. INSTAGRAM DM -- Warm Follow-Up (After they liked/viewed but didn't reply)

These are for people who saw your message, maybe liked it, maybe viewed your story, but never responded. One gentle tap. If they don't reply to this, leave it alone.

### Variation A

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- no worries if you're slammed. Just didn't want my message to get buried. Whenever you have a sec, I'd love your take.

### Variation B

> Totally get it if the timing's off -- just wanted to bump this in case it got lost in the noise. No rush at all.

### Variation C

> Hey -- saw you checked out [my story / my page / the message]. If you're curious at all, happy to send a quick 2-min video walkthrough instead of explaining it all over text. Either way, keep doing your thing.

**Notes:**
- One follow-up. Maximum. If they still don't reply, they are not interested right now. Respect that.
- Never say "just following up" by itself. That phrase is dead. Give them a reason to re-engage.

## 3. FACEBOOK GROUP -- Comment/Introduction

**The rule:** You are there to help, not to sell. Facebook group admins will ban you the second you smell like a pitch. Your job is to be the most useful person in the room. People will check your profile on their own.

### How to introduce yourself (when groups have intro threads)

> Hey everyone -- I'm [YOUR NAME]. I've spent the last [X months/years] building tools for independent martial arts and fitness coaches. Before that I was deep in [relevant background]. Excited to be in here -- always looking to learn from coaches who are actually doing the work. Happy to share anything I've picked up about the business side of coaching if it's ever useful.

### How to comment on someone's question about business tools / organization

> Honestly, the biggest shift for me was getting everything into one place. I was watching coaches juggle Google Sheets for clients, a separate app for scheduling, Venmo for payments, Google Drive for videos -- and none of it connected. The moment you can see your whole business in one view, you make better decisions. Happy to share what I've found works if you want to DM me.

### How to comment on someone struggling with client retention

> Something that made a huge difference for the coaches I work with: a simple weekly check-in. Not a long form. Just a quick "how are you feeling, what's one thing you want to focus on this week." It takes 2 minutes per client and it makes them feel seen. That's what keeps people. Not the programming. The attention.

**Notes:**
- Never drop a link in the group unless someone explicitly asks for it.
- If someone DMs you asking what you built, then you can share. That's permission.
- Post value 3-5 times before you ever mention what you do. Build the reputation first.

## 4. REDDIT -- r/martialarts or r/personaltraining Post

Reddit will downvote you into the ground if you even hint at self-promotion. The approach: be genuinely curious, share real value, let people come to you through your comment history and profile.

### Option A -- Genuine question post

**Title:** Solo martial arts coaches -- how do you keep track of everything?

> Curious how other solo coaches handle the business side. I coach [or: I work with coaches who teach] [DISCIPLINE] and the admin overhead is wild. Client progress tracking, scheduling, payments, video libraries, programming -- it feels like you need 6 different apps and nothing talks to each other.
>
> What's your current setup? Spreadsheets? An app? Just vibes and memory? Genuinely trying to figure out what works and what doesn't.

### Option B -- Value post

**Title:** What I learned helping solo coaches organize their businesses

> Been working with independent martial arts and fitness coaches for a while now, and the same problems keep coming up:
>
> 1. Client info is scattered across 3-5 different tools
> 2. No single place to see how clients are actually progressing
> 3. Hours spent on admin that should be spent coaching
> 4. No clear view of the business (revenue, retention, growth)
>
> The coaches who figured it out all did the same thing: they stopped adding more tools and started consolidating. One place for clients, one place for scheduling, one place for content. The tool matters less than the principle.
>
> If anyone wants to compare notes on what setups actually work, happy to share what I've seen.

### Option C -- Comment on someone else's post about coaching business struggles

> This is so common. Most solo coaches I've talked to are running their business across 5+ disconnected tools and wondering why they feel disorganized. It's not a discipline problem, it's a systems problem. You don't need more willpower, you need fewer tools that actually work together. Happy to share what I've seen work if you want to DM.

**Notes:**
- Never link to your product on Reddit. Ever.
- If someone DMs you asking what you built, share it there. Privately.
- Your Reddit profile bio can mention what you do. People will look.
- Post genuinely useful comments in these subs for weeks before making your own post. Build karma and credibility.

## 5. EMAIL -- Cold Email

For coaches whose email you find on their website, YouTube description, or linktree. Keep it short. Coaches are busy. If they have to scroll, they won't read it.

### Subject line options

1. Quick question about how you run your coaching business
2. Built something for coaches like you -- worth 2 min?
3. [THEIR NAME] -- saw your [THEIR STYLE/DISCIPLINE] content

### Email body (under 150 words)

> Hey [THEIR NAME],
>
> I found you through [WHERE YOU FOUND THEM] and your approach to [THEIR STYLE/DISCIPLINE] stood out.
>
> I'm a solo developer and I've been building an all-in-one platform for independent martial arts and fitness coaches. It replaces the patchwork of spreadsheets, scheduling apps, payment tools, and Google Drive folders that most coaches are currently juggling.
>
> Clients, scheduling, payments, messaging, video library, programs, analytics -- one place.
>
> I'm looking for 10 founding coaches to shape the product and lock in $99/month for life (no setup fee, no contract).
>
> If you're curious, I have two easy options:
>
> -- Watch a 2-min walkthrough: [LOOM LINK]
> -- Book a 15-min call: [CALENDAR LINK]
>
> Either way, appreciate your time.
>
> [YOUR NAME]
> Founder, COACH-OS

**Notes:**
- Send Tuesday through Thursday, 8-10am in their time zone.
- One follow-up email 4 days later if no response. Keep it to 2 sentences: "Hey [NAME] -- just bumping this in case it got buried. Genuinely think it'd be worth a look whenever you have a sec."
- Never send a third email. If they don't respond twice, move on.

## 7. POST-DEMO FOLLOW-UP -- After They've Seen the Product but Haven't Decided

Wait 3-4 days after the demo. They've seen it, they're thinking about it, they haven't pulled the trigger. This message is a gentle nudge with honest urgency -- not manufactured pressure.

### Template

> Subject: Still thinking it over? Totally fair.

> Hey [THEIR NAME],
>
> Just wanted to check in -- no pressure at all. I know switching how you run your business is a big decision, especially when you're already in the middle of coaching [X] clients.
>
> I keep thinking about what you said about [SPECIFIC THING FROM THEIR CALL -- e.g., "losing track of which clients need check-ins" or "spending Sunday nights updating spreadsheets"]. That's exactly the kind of thing that disappears once everything's in one place.
>
> Wanted to be upfront: [X] of the 10 founding spots are left. I'm not going to pretend they're disappearing tomorrow, but I am talking to a few other coaches this week. Once they fill, the price goes to the standard rate and the direct-access-to-me part goes away.
>
> If you want to jump in: [SIGNUP LINK]
> If you have more questions: just reply here or text me at [YOUR NUMBER]
> If the timing's just not right: completely understand. The door's always open.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

**Notes:**
- Only send this once. If they don't respond, add them to your email list (with permission) and let content marketing do the work over time.
- Never guilt trip. Never say "I'm surprised I haven't heard from you." Respect their timeline.

## 8. REFERRAL ASK -- After a Coach Signs Up

Wait until they have been using the platform for at least 1-2 weeks and have had a positive experience. Do not ask for referrals on day one. That feels transactional.

### Template

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- really glad the platform is working well for you. Quick question: is there another coach or two in your circle who might be dealing with the same headaches you were before? I've still got [X] founding spots at $99/month and I'd rather fill them with coaches who come recommended by people I already trust. No pressure to make intros -- even just a name and I'll reach out respectfully. Appreciate you either way.

### If they say yes

> Awesome -- really appreciate that. If it's easier, you can just drop their Instagram handle or email and I'll reach out with a quick note mentioning you sent me their way. I won't be pushy, promise.

### If they refer someone and that person signs up

> Hey [THEIR NAME] -- just wanted to let you know [REFERRAL NAME] signed up. That intro meant a lot. If there's anything I can do to make your experience even better, just say the word. You're helping me build something real and I don't forget that.

**Notes:**
- A warm intro converts 5-10x better than cold outreach. This is your highest-leverage channel once you have even a few happy users.
- Consider building a simple referral reward later (free month, feature request priority, etc.). For now, genuine gratitude is enough.

## 9. LOOM VIDEO SCRIPT -- 2-Minute Product Walkthrough

Record this screenshare-style with your face in the corner. Be natural. Talk like you're showing a friend, not presenting to investors.

### OPENING -- Who you are (10 seconds)

> "Hey -- I'm [YOUR NAME]. I'm a solo developer and I built COACH-OS specifically for independent martial arts and fitness coaches. Not gyms, not big studios -- solo coaches who run everything themselves."

### PROBLEM -- What coaches deal with (15 seconds)

> "Here's what I kept seeing: coaches with 10, 15, 20 clients, and every single one of them is managing their business across a patchwork of tools. Google Sheets for client tracking. A scheduling app. Venmo or Stripe for payments. Google Drive for videos. WhatsApp for messaging. And none of it connects. So you're spending hours on admin instead of actually coaching."

### SOLUTION -- Quick tour of key features (60 seconds)

> "So here's what COACH-OS looks like when you log in."
>
> [Show dashboard]
> "This is your dashboard. Every client, every session, every dollar -- one view. You can see at a glance who needs attention, who's progressing, who's falling off."
>
> [Show client profile]
> "Each client has a full profile. Their goals, their history, their program, their check-ins. You're not digging through folders to remember where someone's at."
>
> [Show scheduling]
> "Scheduling is built in. Clients book with you, it syncs to your calendar, they get reminders. No more back-and-forth texts."
>
> [Show video library]
> "This is the video library. Upload your technique breakdowns, drills, conditioning work -- organize it however you want and assign videos directly to clients or programs."
>
> [Show analytics -- if ready]
> "And this is the part most coaches never had before -- actual analytics. Revenue, retention, client progress over time. You can finally see if your business is growing or just busy."

### CLOSE -- Founding member offer (15 seconds)

> "Right now I'm looking for 10 founding coaches. $99 a month, locked in for life, no setup fee, no contract. You get the platform plus direct access to me -- I'm building this with you, not just for you. If you're interested, there's a link below to book a 15-minute call or just reply to whoever sent you this. Appreciate your time."

### Production notes
- Keep it under 2:30 total. Aim for 2:00.
- Record in one take if possible. Minor stumbles are fine -- they make it feel real.
- Have the product loaded and ready before you hit record. No waiting for pages to load.
- Your face should be visible in the bottom corner the entire time.
- At the end, add a text overlay with: "Book a call: [CALENDAR LINK]" and "[X] founding spots left."

## Quick Reference -- Outreach Rules

1. **Personalize everything.** If you can't say something specific about them, don't reach out yet. Do the research first.
2. **One follow-up maximum** on cold outreach. Two if you count the original. After that, silence is the answer.
3. **Never pitch in public spaces.** Facebook groups, Reddit comments, forum threads -- these are for helping, not selling.
4. **Let people come to you.** The best outreach doesn't feel like outreach. It feels like someone useful showed up.
5. **Track every conversation.** Use a simple spreadsheet: Name, Platform, Date Contacted, Status, Notes. Do not rely on memory.
6. **The founding member offer is real.** Do not manufacture urgency. If spots are actually filling, say so honestly. If they are not, do not pretend.
7. **Your reputation is your funnel.** Every interaction is either building trust or breaking it. There is no neutral.
