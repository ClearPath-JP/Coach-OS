# DM Templates — Founding Coach Outreach

Instructions: Fill in every [bracket]. Keep it short. Send as a voice note if you know them well — it converts better than text. Never paste a link in the first message.

---

## WARM DMs (people you know / met IRL)

### Warm 1 — The direct check-in (best for coaches you have trained with or know personally)

> Hey [Name], hope training is going well. Quick question — how are you managing the business side of your coaching right now? Like client tracking, scheduling, getting paid? Genuinely curious what you are using.

*Wait for their answer. Then respond naturally based on what they say. If they describe chaos or multiple tools, that is your opening.*

---

### Warm 2 — The "I built something" opener (for people who know you coach)

> Hey [Name] — I have been building something for coaches like us. A tool that replaces the whole notebook + spreadsheet + Venmo mess with one place. Client notes, scheduling, group classes, billing — all of it. Would love your honest feedback. Want me to walk you through it?

---

### Warm 3 — The referral ask (when you know a coach who might know a coach)

> Hey [Name] — I know this might not be your thing, but do you know any solo martial arts or fitness coaches who are growing a private client practice? I built a business tool specifically for that situation and I am looking for a few founding coaches to test it before it goes public. The founding rate is locked in at $99/mo for life. Just thought of you because of the coaches you know.

---

## COLD DMs (IG, text, or first contact with someone you do not know)

### Cold 1 — Lead with teaching, no product mention

> Hey [Name] — I came across your page and your [specific thing: BJJ classes / conditioning work / whatever]. Quick tip I share with private coaches: if you are running clients through a WhatsApp group, the first thing that breaks is accountability. People forget their homework and blame you, not the tool. Happy to share what I do instead if it would be useful.

*If they engage, share the insight naturally. Kindo comes up only if they ask what you use.*

---

### Cold 2 — Specific observation opener

> Hey [Name] — I coach [martial art / conditioning] too, out of [your setup]. I noticed you are running [X clients / weekly classes / etc.] — how are you managing the scheduling and payment side? That was the thing that nearly buried me before I sorted it out.

*Pure curiosity. No product. Let them tell their story first.*

---

### Cold 3 — Founding invitation (only after 1–2 exchanges, never cold first message)

> I should mention — I am actually the person who built Kindo, a business OS for solo coaches like us. I am bringing on a handful of founding coaches at a locked rate before it opens publicly. It covers client tracking, scheduling, group classes, memberships, billing — the whole backend. If you ever want to see it, I will walk you through it live, no pitch, just show you what it does. No pressure either way.

---

## FOLLOW-UP NUDGES

### Follow-up 1 — If they went quiet after showing interest (send 5–7 days later)

> Hey [Name] — no rush on my end, just wanted to make sure my last message did not get buried. Still happy to do a quick walkthrough whenever it works for you. The founding rate is still open for now.

---

### Follow-up 2 — If they said "maybe later" or "sounds interesting" and then nothing

> Hey — I know you mentioned maybe later, and that is completely fair. The one thing worth knowing is that the $99 founding rate closes when I hit my first 10 founding coaches. Not a pressure thing, just want to make sure you have the info. If the timing is still off, no worries — just reach out when it makes sense.

---

## Notes on using these

- Voice notes outperform text with warm contacts. Read the warm templates out loud, naturally.
- Never send a link in the first message. Let curiosity pull them.
- If someone says "I just use a notebook," see the objections doc — do not argue, just share the insight.
- Cold DMs 1 and 2 are pure relationship-building. Only bring up Kindo when they ask or you have had a real conversation.
